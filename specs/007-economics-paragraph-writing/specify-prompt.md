# `/speckit-specify` prompt for spec 007

Paste everything inside the block below into your agent as one message. The first line makes Spec Kit
write `spec.md` into this folder (beside `design.md` and `mockups/`) instead of creating a new one.

```text
/speckit-specify SPECIFY_FEATURE_DIRECTORY=specs/007-economics-paragraph-writing

Economics Paragraph Writing: a new Writing Practice unit (unit id y12-economics-paragraph-writing) that teaches Year 12 students to write a Band 6 economics body paragraph one part at a time, using the five-part framework: Topic Sentence, Theoretical Mechanism, Evidence & Application, Evaluation & Limitation, Linking Judgement. It is the paragraph-level sequel to the sentence-writing unit (spec 005, with the question layout from spec 006) and reuses its approach: one unaided try marked by an LLM, one correction worth less, a discussion with a model answer, and missed items feeding "Things I need to know". Why: students lose marks in extended responses on structure (describing instead of judging, skipping steps in the chain, dumping data, naming a drawback without weighing it, ending with something new), and their first task that tests this is the Year 12 Economics Assessment Task 1 on Tue 1 Dec 2026.

The visual and wording reference is specs/007-economics-paragraph-writing/design.md, with mockups in specs/007-economics-paragraph-writing/mockups/ (desktop and phone for each of the five screens). Read both first. Use the exact on-screen wording from design.md, and put the visual details (sizes, spacing, colours) into the plan, not the spec. The settled requirements are in the Claude doc "Economics Paragraph Writing — Requirements (draft spec 007)" (https://claude.ai/artifact/G39xpYVQPsg6qWvqpyERdo); the marker pilot is in resources/topic010/ and scripts/pilot-paragraph-marker.py.

Terms: a part is one of the five items of the framework; together they make one body paragraph. An essay is one essay question with the content for that paragraph. A quiz is one question: one part of one essay.

How a student moves through the unit:
- One part per quiz, in framework order. The student's next quiz continues the same essay with the next part; after the Link, the next quiz starts the next essay. One essay is in progress at a time: the unit page shows it (for example "Essay 3 of 12 · next: Evidence & Application", the essay question, five part dots with the recorded mark on each finished part) and its button always continues it ("Continue essay (Part 3 of 5)"). No skipping a part and no restarting mid-essay.
- Each question shows: the essay's five parts as the quiz progress bar; the essay question quoted in full with its directive verb highlighted and defined; "What this part must do" (plain lines taken from that part's criteria, so the rubric is taught before writing); the brief (the paragraph's content in plain English dot points: economy and strategy, argument, counterpoint, chain steps, facts, limitation), with every field visible and the fields this part needs marked "Use in this part"; the model text of the earlier parts of this essay, whatever the student scored on them, with a slot for this part; and a text box with a word meter.
- Later parts' model text is never shown before the student writes them, including at the discussion: the discussion shows the model paragraph up to and including this part, with this part highlighted and the later parts as labelled "next quiz" slots.
- The brief supplies every fact and figure; the student does the writing. Every idea in a model part is already in the brief: the model adds structure, precision and terminology, never new facts.

Marking:
- Each part is marked out of 4: three criteria specific to the part plus one shared conventions criterion (precise economic language; within the part's word limit with no padding), each met or not met. The criteria per part are in the requirements doc and in the round 5 marker. Cause-and-effect connectors are not marked; the feedback may still mention them.
- Word limits per part: Topic Sentence 40, Mechanism 90, Evidence 80, Evaluation 90, Link 45. Going over loses the conciseness half of the conventions criterion and never blocks submission.
- The counterpoint thread: the Topic Sentence must frame the counterpoint, the Evaluation must discuss the same limitation the topic sentence framed, and the Link must weigh argument against counterpoint and add nothing new. "The topic sentence" here means the model Topic Sentence shown to the student, not their own earlier attempt.
- Phase 1: the student submits; the result shows the mark out of 4, a met/not met verdict and one feedback line for every criterion, and the first try. A 4/4 goes straight to the discussion.
- Phase 2 (only under 4): one correction, starting from the first try in the box, with one tip card per lost criterion naming the criterion and its writing tip. The tips never contain model text. The correction is marked on the same criteria and capped at 3; there is no third try and it cannot be skipped. The recorded mark is the higher of phase 1 and the capped correction, and the discussion explains the cap.
- Discussion: the recorded-mark summary; per-criterion results each marked "First try" or "Fixed in correction" (or not met); a "This mark seems wrong" button; the student's final text beside the model part; the model paragraph so far with this part highlighted; the tips added to "Things I need to know" (phase 1 misses only); the first try collapsed; "Finish", naming the next part.
- While a part is being marked the student's text stays on screen, locked, with timed status steps; on an error the editable box returns holding their text.
- The grader is built from the round 5 prompt in scripts/pilot-paragraph-marker.py. It receives the brief, the earlier parts shown to the student, the part's criteria and the marking notes, and returns per-criterion verdicts, a one-line reason for each, an overall feedback line, and the tips missed.

"Things I need to know": the unit's concepts are 17 writing tips, one per criterion (three per part plus the two shared conventions tips); the connectors advice is feedback only and never tracked. A tip missed in phase 1 appears with its one-line advice and progress pips, carries across attempts, and clears after 2 correct phase 1 uses for a part tip and 3 for an all-parts tip. Only phase 1 counts: a tip fixed in the correction still counts as missed.

Revision from real use (the marker matched 46 of 50 tuning answers and 21 of 25 held-out answers; every held-out miss was leniency):
- Store each part's text for each phase, the per-criterion verdicts and the marker's one-line reason for each, with the attempt and the prompt version that marked it.
- The "This mark seems wrong" button flags that answer for review. It never changes the mark, and an answer can be flagged once.
- A revision loop: flagged answers, once reviewed, become labelled test cases in resources/topic010/pilot-essays.json (as a "field" set), and every prompt change reruns every case in that file, so fixing one answer cannot silently break another. The prompt version is bumped on every change. The frozen India held-out set stays in the file and the prompt is never tuned to it.
- Because students rarely flag a mark that is too generous: a periodic sample of real full-mark answers is re-marked offline by a stronger model, and disagreements become labelled test cases. Check the three leniency patterns in resources/topic010/06-findings-holdout.md first: comparatives taken as a judgement in the Link; "success" accepted as the named outcome in the Topic Sentence; a drawback inside the Mechanism with no statistic.
- Reviewing flags and running the re-mark are offline teacher tasks (scripts); a teacher review page is not part of v1.

Degrade path: if the marker is unavailable or the student is over LLM_DAILY_CAP, the model part is shown and the student ticks each criterion they met; the ticks give the mark and the tip results. There is no phase 2 on this path. No error is shown and no mark is invented.

Limits and logging: one LLM call per phase, at most 2 per quiz, counted against LLM_DAILY_CAP. Grading logs only minimal metrics (as spec 005 FR-044), never answer text.

Content: 12 original essays for v1, each with a brief, five model parts that together form one Band 6 paragraph, per-part "What this part must do" lines, which brief fields each part uses, per-part marking notes and a commentary line per model part. First topic: the Global Economy, aligned to the Assessment Task 1 notification (globalisation's influence on an emerging and a developing economy; strategies since 1980 to promote growth and development). Essay questions are general ("an emerging economy you have studied"); each brief uses one well-documented economy (proposed: Vietnam, China, India, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Ethiopia, two essays each on different strategies). Directive verbs vary (evaluate, assess, analyse, discuss). No source line (constitution Principle V). Not every essay must exist before 1 Dec 2026: the unit launches with the first essays and grows. Figures shown in design.md and the mockups are placeholders; the content task supplies and verifies them. Content validation checks every essay has all five parts, every model part is within its word limit, and the topic sentence's counterpoint matches the evaluation's.

Constitution: this feature needs amendments, made in this feature: Principle I, to release to the browser, before phase 1 is marked, the brief, the "What this part must do" lines and the model text of earlier parts of the essay, the per-criterion verdicts and tips after phase 1, and the current part's model at the discussion or self-mark screen, and never later parts' model text or the marking notes; Principle II, to store the per-criterion verdicts and reasons, the prompt version and the flag; and the derived concept-count rule, for part tips that clear after 2.

Resume: resuming mid-question shows the state the attempt is in (the correction with its tip cards and the saved text, or the discussion); marking states are never restored and no typed text is lost. A part left without a recorded mark is offered again next quiz.

Accessibility: verdicts and statuses are never shown by colour alone; marking progress is announced through a live status region; the result summary takes focus when it appears; animations stop under prefers-reduced-motion; touch targets at least 44 px; works at 390 px with no sideways scrolling.

Out of scope for v1: whole essays, introductions and conclusions; diagrams; quoting Section III stimulus; photo answers; teacher reports and a teacher page for flags; changes to other units.

Tests: an essay advances one part per quiz in order and only one is in progress; earlier parts' model text appears on the writing screen and later parts never reach the browser before they are written, including at the discussion; the current part's model is not released before phase 1 is marked; per-part word limits and the conciseness rule; the correction starts from the first try, is capped at 3, cannot be skipped and has no third try; the recorded mark is the higher try; tip cards never contain model text; tip tracking counts phase 1 only, part tips clear after 2 and all-parts tips after 3; verdicts, reasons and prompt version are stored with the attempt; the flag is stored once and never changes the mark; at most 2 LLM calls per quiz against LLM_DAILY_CAP; the self-mark path (no phase 2) when the marker fails or the cap is used; logs carry no answer text; content validation rules; the marker regression run covers every case in pilot-essays.json.

Must comply with constitution 2.13.0 (as amended by this feature).
```
