Run batch 0 first (the concept list and the figures file); it does not depend on the marker.

```text
Spec 010 content task, BATCH <0|1|2>. Build the content of the Economics essay-writing unit (y12-economics-essay-writing) for NSW HSC Economics Year 12, Topic 1 The Global Economy: the influence of globalisation on an emerging and a developing economy, and the strategies (since 1980) used to promote growth and development. The student's economies are China (emerging) and Nepal (developing). Read first: specs/010-economics-essay-writing/spec.md (FR-029, FR-038 to FR-047), research.md (R2, R9, R13), data-model.md (§1, §5), lib/essay/rubric.json (the AT1 band descriptors, the five criteria and the marker's reply) and lib/essay/concepts.json (the concept list).

Batches (process only the current batch, then stop for review):
- Batch 0: check and extend the concept list in lib/essay/concepts.json: confirm each of the 26 entries is accurate and distinct, say which a Band 1 essay on each of the six questions would use, and propose additions only where an essay on these questions would clearly need a concept the list lacks (keep keys short, `china-…`, `nepal-…` or `skill-…`; never rename an existing key). Create resources/topic011/figures.md: one table per economy of the contemporary figures the essays may use (GDP growth, GDP per capita, HDI, Gini, poverty rate, emissions, remittances share of GDP, FDI, trade share, tourism, hydropower exports), each with the figure, its year, the source (publisher, table or release, URL) and the date you checked it. Use sources a student could name: NBS China, World Bank, IMF, UNDP, Nepal Rastra Bank, Nepal Central Bureau of Statistics, WTO, UNCTAD, UN. Mark any figure you could not verify as UNVERIFIED and do not use it.
- Batch 1: essays e1 (China, Barker 2024 Trial Q27), e3 (Nepal, Sydney Boys 2020 Trial Q27) and e5 (both, new).
- Batch 2: essays e2 (China, new), e4 (Nepal, Abbotsleigh 2025 Trial Q28) and e6 (both, new).

The questions (verbatim for trial essays; never reword):
- e1 (China): "With reference to an economy other than Australia, explain the role that globalisation has played in their economic growth and development." Source: Barker 2024 Trial, Q27. Verb: explain.
- e2 (China): "Assess the effectiveness of the strategies used by an emerging economy you have studied to balance economic growth with environmental sustainability and income equality." New. Verb: assess.
- e3 (Nepal): "Evaluate the impacts of globalisation on the growth and development of an economy other than Australia." Source: Sydney Boys 2020 Trial, Q27. Verb: evaluate.
- e4 (Nepal): "Explain the impact of globalisation in achieving economic growth and development on an economy other than Australia." Source: Abbotsleigh 2025 Trial, Q28. Verb: explain.
- e5 (both): "Evaluate the effectiveness of the strategies used to promote economic growth and development in one emerging and one developing economy you have studied." New. Verb: evaluate.
- e6 (both): "Assess the extent to which integration into the global economy has improved living standards in one emerging and one developing economy you have studied." New. Verb: assess.

For each essay in the batch produce, as a TypeScript object in the shape of EssayKey (data-model.md §1) written to resources/topic011/content/<essay id>.json (the unit's answers.ts is generated from these after review; do not edit the app code):
1. schoolCriteria (trial essays e1, e3, e4 only; null for the others). Open the school's paper and solutions: resources/y12_economics_trial_exams/ (text extracts in .cline/trials/). Copy the school's marking criteria for that question VERBATIM (every band or mark descriptor and every "indicative content" line), plus the school's sample answer's key points if the solutions give one. Do not paraphrase, shorten or add. Record the file, the page and the question number. If the solutions give none, set null and say so.
2. markingNotes: what a strong answer to THIS question (its directive verb, its economy rule: e1 and e2 China, e3 and e4 Nepal, e5 and e6 both economies) is likely to cover: the strategies and globalisation influences since 1980 with dates and actors (government, IMF, World Bank, WTO, donors); the models that fit (AD/AS, PPF, Lorenz curve, Harrod-Domar, comparative advantage); the indicators with years (from figures.md only); the judgement the question calls for and the counterpoints worth weighing; how the five criteria (understanding, theory, assessment, data, structure) show up; what the marker should NOT penalise (other valid content earns equal credit); common misconceptions. 250 to 450 words, plain prose with short labelled lines. Do not copy the school's criteria into it.
3. modelEssay: a Band 1 (17 to 20) essay of 600 to 800 words (count it with whitespace-separated tokens: a hyphenated word is one word), in the voice of a strong Year 12 student: an introduction that states the judgement and defines key terms; three or four body paragraphs each with a point, a model or theory used in words, indicators with years from figures.md, an evaluation (a limitation or counterpoint) and a link back to the judgement; a conclusion that restates the judgement and names the reason that weighs most. For e5 and e6 treat BOTH economies. It must answer the directive verb (explain: cause and effect, why and how; assess or evaluate: a judgement of effectiveness or extent). It must cover growth, development, environmental sustainability and income equality where the question allows. No Australia. Every figure must be in figures.md, with its year, and quoted as the source says (a rounded figure is fine; never an invented one). End with a "Sources" line listing the sources of the figures used (it is not counted in the words and is not shown to students).
4. concepts: the concept keys from the list a Band 1 answer to this question would use (3 to 10 keys, most important first).

Then:
5. Check every model essay with the real marker: run it through the marker (python scripts/pilot-essay-marker.py with the essay added as a labelled case, expected band 1, 3 runs). Every model essay must reach band 1 on the majority of runs, with no data flags, and its gaps (if any) must be concepts on the list. Revise any that fail and list them.
6. Run npm run validate (it checks word counts, concept keys and key presence for essays marked live), npm test and npx tsc --noEmit through Git Bash.
7. Stop and give me: for each essay the school criteria (verbatim, with file and page), the marking notes, the model essay with its word count, the concept keys, every figure used with its year and source (from figures.md), anything you could not verify, and each marker result line (runs, bands, flags). Do not set `live`: I approve each essay and the marker gate decides.

Rules: school criteria and sample answers stay verbatim and credited (constitution Principle V: they are server-only and used only as the question's rubric); the original PDFs and run reports stay in gitignored resources/; do not use any real HSC question or past-paper text; do not invent figures, sources or quotations; no new runtime dependency; use .cline/ for scratch scripts (tmp-<purpose>.<ext>) and delete them at the end; use Git Bash for every shell command.
```
